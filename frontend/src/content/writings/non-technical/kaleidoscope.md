I have been stalling this for quite some time now. It has been a while since I sat down with my thoughts and channeled the chaos that’s inside my head to see if some patterns emerge from what always feels like a kaleidoscope. The article is named so because I do have faith that, despite the irregularities, there must be something that gives structure to it. Symmetry is the best compressor, and one of the ways to bring order to something that has no order is to just mirror it. That way, you now have two random patterns, and despite not having any pattern individually, the overall construction still does! Believe it or not, I had thought about naming the first article “Kaleidoscope” long ago — maybe because I wanted it to be unique — but I could never decide on the content.

At one point, I wanted it to be about people in my life. But then I did not want it to be specifically about anyone. After all, who should I write about? The most important people in my life are quite unarguably my friends and family, which is a big list most certainly. Any specification to one would be unjust to others because, while there might be a slight hierarchy in my relationship with them, I do hold all of them in the same 12x8x6 cm heart of mine. Along with that, a better argument for not writing about them might be the fact that I have all of them in my life. We are growing together, living our lives every day, making new memories both with and without the company of each other, but there is this strange feeling that I have which always asks me to delay writing about them because it would feel like an epilogue. Finally, not to mention, I am no Walter Isaacson or Tagore; I am not gifted with the ability to pen down — more like type down, because I am a zoomer — in any reasonable way about the brilliant people in my life. I don’t want them to go through the embarrassment of any of their acquaintances finding out about my blog.

Another idea that I have had since my final year in college — the time when I began maintaining my website for grad school — was to write about the people who have had a short stint in my life, but a very impactful one. I don’t know if I should call these people friends or not because, while at one point they were friends of mine, unfortunately, now I happen to have lost touch with them. Some I could still reach out to, and some I cannot, but either way, owing to my introverted nature, I think I would rather let them be at peace without disturbing them. I will explain this behavior in the next paragraph. Concluding my point about “temporary” people in my life, all I pray for is that they do well wherever they are. I know they will do well without my calls or letters; proof of that could be the fact that they have already done well without my calls or letters. I do think about them, and as an overthinker, I do feel insecure about whether they remember me. I happen to have a good memory when it comes to life incidents or people. I remember acts of kindness, I remember words of empathy, and more importantly, I remember people. I remember how they, at one point, made me feel.

I must acknowledge my own stupid and toxic behavior of not reaching out to friends, or so I have been accused of. I don’t think I do that out of any ill intent at all. In my defense, I think this behavior stems from not wanting to disturb people. I often feel my calls might disturb them, the “temporary” or “used to know” ones. I prefer texting over phone calls anyway. I think it’s more elegant because it gives people the option to respond when they want to, and I just believe that in certain contexts, it’s more efficient than calling someone up — which feels like a confrontation at times — and asking what is going on in life. I think what I am trying to say is that people like me prefer being given time to process interaction. At times, it’s also helpful in coming up with lies or alibis for whatever the “confrontation” is going to be about. Is it overthinking when it could very well just be a regular check-in call? Absolutely. I do not deny the toxicity, but it’s just an excuse I have come up with, I guess, to keep sweeping the dirt under the rug whenever applicable.

Let me also make it very clear that I have never wanted to knowingly drive people away. I try my best to resume the interactions in good faith, and one of the things that I am very proud of is that if someone tries to reach out to me, I always engage. It makes me happy that people remember. Somewhere between remembering everything and everyone and not wanting to disturb them, I make an inherent assumption that they also might have forgotten all about me. Being proven wrong in this way has got to be one of the most satisfying things ever. Thankfully, a lot of such people exist in my life who have upgraded their “temporary” statuses just by being there and making efforts. I am grateful for them, and I leave no stone unturned in order to reciprocate the efforts they put into building a good relationship with me.

The above discourse serves the point I am trying to make in this article — my way of thinking. I have tried hard to understand how my thoughts work. Being an observer of your own self is very fatal, I say that from my experience. But I do see patterns, and therefore, I am hopeful that my brain won’t self-destruct. I have been given this feedback by many people: that I keep context-switching mid-conversation. Prof. DC jokingly used to say, “Abhinav, finish your sentences!” And he was right; I won’t contest. My brain is always running quite a few possible threads at the same time, not knowing which is the best branch to explore, and that’s what makes me sometimes wait and think before committing to something — basically evaluating all other possibilities — which is not bad because slow and deep thinkers do the same thing all the time. The difference is that they don’t do it mid-conversation like I do. This is so because I idolize fast thinkers. My education has been such that I had to think fast and accurately, which is the most optimal thing to do in almost any situation. The exception is research, which I believe benefits from deep thought, not necessarily slow thought, which sometimes is required to be illogical, as exploration is often driven by uncertainty. I am not a great researcher, and this is one of the reasons why I feel I might never be — or maybe I don’t want to be. Anyway, I hardly pay attention to actively mastering one type of thinking. It’s futile, in my opinion. I try to adapt as and when needed, and I am sure I will continue in this way for very long.

When I started writing this evening, I had no idea what I was going to write about. I had to make this special; after all, it was my ‘first’ article! But now, after spending a few hours on this, I am realizing I should not push for perfection when it isn’t necessary. Imperfections are asymmetric, and hence do not compress information, but they do us a bigger favor — they make us unique. As the saying goes in Silicon Valley, “just make it exist first; you can make it perfect later.” Now that I have at least started writing, it’s a long way to go to come anywhere near writing useful stuff like Paul Graham or Sam Altman; they are far more experienced and intelligent than me. But as aforementioned, their imperfections are different from mine, and it’s natural for that to be reflected in our writing.

I feel confident and determined that what little value I create is worth sharing on my blog henceforth. If that means writing for a nonexistent audience — straight into the void — then so be it.
